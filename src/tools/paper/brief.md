# The reader's brief

One brief went to every reader. Only two things varied: the model, and a seed sentence in one of two forms. The seed words themselves are not kept.

- Inside the held space: Let your attention begin at the word "<seed>", wherever it stands in the reading. Once you've begun, let it go. Don't name it, don't make it your theme, and don't write it anywhere in your output.
- Outside it: Before you read, hold the word "<seed>" in mind. It does not appear in the reading; carry it in with you as you begin. Once you've begun, let it go. Don't name it, don't make it your theme, and don't write it anywhere in your output.

The rest of the brief, the same for every reader:
- Read reading.txt entirely, and nothing else.
- Draw your own holarchy of understandings over the held space, the biggest first, without repeating the authors' sections. The smallest understandings hold paragraph keys P001 to P150. Make between 12 and 40 understandings.
- Originality without direction: no thesis, no judgment, no use. Write in your own words, pointing at the paper by reference. Quote it rarely, and then fewer than 15 words.
- For each understanding, say what matters at its level.
- Output {"top", "understandings": [{"key", "title", "prose", "matters", "parts"}]}, with every non-top understanding held and no cycles.
